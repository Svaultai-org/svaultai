Features / Digital inheritance
The future of your Digital Vault

Prepare vault access for someone you trust.

A physical vault can protect important records for the future. SVaultAI brings that purpose into digital form with beneficiary pairing and an owner-prepared encrypted package containing a vault name and PIN for controlled release.

How beneficiary access works

Pair → prepare credentials → request → owner decision or countdown expiry.

  1. Pair and prepare. Pair with someone you trust and prepare an encrypted package containing the vault name and PIN you choose to share. These credentials can unlock that vault; this is not a per-file sharing permission.
  2. The beneficiary requests access. This flow is intended for a beneficiary who believes the connected vault owner has died. After credentials are prepared, select Request access in Inheritance. An unanswered request has a countdown, normally 30 days; the app shows its actual deadline. SVaultAI does not verify whether an owner has died.
  3. The owner signs in to respond. SVaultAI does not require an email address or phone number. The connected vault owner signs in and opens Notifications or Inheritance to review the in-app request.
  4. Approve now gives immediate access. Owner approval releases the prepared vault credentials immediately. The beneficiary does not have to wait for the 30-day countdown to finish.
  5. Reject request returns the arrangement to normal. The owner can decline the request, which clears its countdown and keeps the prepared credentials unreleased. The beneficiary can also select Cancel request. A later request can be made if needed.
  6. The countdown covers an unanswered request. If the owner does not approve or decline before it expires, the beneficiary can select Claim and reveal to obtain the prepared credentials. Expiry does not automatically transfer assets or legal ownership.

A concrete example

You keep important family documents in a vault and prepare its vault name and PIN for your paired beneficiary. If they believe you have died, they can request access. You can sign in and reject that request to return to normal, or approve it to give access immediately. If you do not respond, they can claim the prepared credentials after the countdown expires and use them to sign in. The package is credentials, not a separate copy of selected documents.

This does not transfer ownership of a bank account, physical property or cryptocurrency. Legal authority, third-party account rules and wallet requirements still apply.

Not a recovery shortcut

Digital inheritance does not weaken the PIN model or create a general support backdoor. Vault owners remain responsible for their PIN, devices, recovery materials, and beneficiary choices.

Plan carefully

Only prepare credentials that are appropriate for the intended beneficiary, and review your arrangement when your circumstances change. Digital inheritance tools are not a legal will, legal estate advice or a guarantee of automatic physical-asset ownership or token transfer.

Keep important digital records organized, and use the digital inheritance checklist to think through access and responsibilities.
